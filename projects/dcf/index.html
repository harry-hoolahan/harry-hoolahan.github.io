How the model works

Revenue grows from the base year at a rate that moves in a straight line from the Year 1 rate to the Year 5 rate. EBIT is a fixed share of revenue. Free cash flow is EBIT after tax, plus depreciation and amortisation, minus capital expenditure, minus the investment in working capital needed to support the extra revenue.

Each year's free cash flow is discounted at WACC. Terminal value is Year 5 free cash flow, grown once at the terminal growth rate and divided by WACC minus that growth rate. Enterprise value is the present value of the five forecast cash flows plus the present value of the terminal value. Subtract net debt for equity value, then divide by shares.

What it leaves out

  • Cash flows arrive at year end. A mid-year convention would raise value slightly.
  • Tax is a flat rate on EBIT. There are no tax losses, deferred tax or interest tax shield.
  • Terminal free cash flow reuses Year 5 capital intensity. A real model would normalise capex against depreciation and working capital against terminal growth.
  • There are no leases, pensions, minorities or share-based pay in the bridge from enterprise value to equity value.
  • WACC is an input here, not built up from a cost of equity and cost of debt.

The calculation is in one short file and is checked by automated tests, including a hand calculation and a case with a known closed-form answer.
